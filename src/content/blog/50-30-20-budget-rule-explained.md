The 50/30/20 budget rule is popular because it fits on a sticky note. Half of your take-home pay goes to needs, 30% to wants, and 20% to savings and extra debt payments. No categories to maintain, no spreadsheet. The catch is that the percentages were never meant to fit everyone, and for many people rent alone breaks the first number.

This guide works through the rule with real-looking numbers, shows where the lines between needs and wants get blurry, and gives you a version for months when 50% is not realistic. We use plain units rather than a currency, so the maths works wherever you live.

## The rule in one table

The split is applied to **take-home pay**, the amount that actually lands in your account after tax and deductions.

| Bucket | Share | What goes in it |
|---|---|---|
| Needs | 50% | Housing, utilities, groceries, transport to work, insurance, minimum loan payments |
| Wants | 30% | Eating out, hobbies, subscriptions, travel, upgrades beyond the basics |
| Savings and debt | 20% | Emergency fund, retirement, payments above the minimum |

The rule was popularised in a 2005 personal finance book and has been repeated ever since. It is a rule of thumb, not an official standard. Official guidance, such as the US Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's [budgeting guide](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/blog/budgeting-how-to-create-a-budget-and-stick-with-it/), starts from your own income and spending rather than fixed percentages.

## A worked example

Say your take-home pay is 2,400 units a month.

| Bucket | Target share | Target amount | Actual spending (example) | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Needs | 50% | 1,200 | 1,380 | 180 over |
| Wants | 30% | 720 | 610 | 110 under |
| Savings and debt | 20% | 480 | 410 | 70 under |
| Total | 100% | 2,400 | 2,400 | |

The "actual" column is what makes the rule useful. Here, needs run 180 over. Most of that is rent, which you cannot change this month. The honest move is not to feel bad about it. It is to take the 180 from wants, so savings stay close to target.

Here is the same example rebalanced:

| Bucket | Rebalanced share | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Needs | 57.5% | 1,380 |
| Wants | 22.5% | 540 |
| Savings and debt | 20% | 480 |

That is still a sound budget. It just is not 50/30/20.

## Needs or wants? The blurry cases

| Item | Usually | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Basic phone plan | Need | You need to be reachable for work and family |
| Newest phone on instalments | Want, mostly | The phone is a need, the upgrade is a want |
| Groceries | Need | |
| Takeaway three times a week | Want | Even if it feels necessary on busy weeks |
| Car to get to work | Need | If there is no realistic public transport |
| Gym membership | Want | Unless a doctor has prescribed it |
| Minimum loan payment | Need | Missing it costs more |
| Extra loan payment | Savings and debt | It belongs in the 20% |

When you are unsure, ask: if I lost my income tomorrow, would I keep paying this? If yes, it is probably a need.

## When 50% for needs is not realistic

In many cities, rent alone takes 40% or more of take-home pay. In that case, the rule still helps as a shape:

- **Try 60/20/20.** Needs take more, wants shrink, savings stay protected.
- **If that fails, try 70/20/10** for a few months, with a date to review it.
- **Protect a small savings number above all.** Even 5% builds the habit and a small cushion.

The CFPB's one-page [monthly budget form](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_well-being_monthly-budget.pdf) is a good way to find out what your real split is before you pick a target.

## Tracking the split in Unutma

Unutma does not have a "50/30/20 mode", and we would rather say that up front. You can still track it with what is there:

1. In the Budget (Economy) tab, log income and expenses as usual, using categories such as Groceries, Bills, Transport, Entertainment and Food.
2. Under "Monthly Budget", tap Set Budget and add a limit to the categories that belong to wants, such as Entertainment and Food.
3. At the end of the month, open "By category" and add up which categories were needs and which were wants.
4. Log savings as their own entries so the 20% is visible, or create a target under "Savings Goals".

It is manual. Everything stays on your phone, and nothing is connected to your bank. Our [expense tracker without bank linking](/blog/unutma-economy-expenses-budgets-assets/) guide walks through each of these screens.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the 50/30/20 rule based on gross or net income?

Net, meaning take-home pay after tax and deductions. Using gross income makes every bucket look bigger than it really is.

### Does debt go in needs or savings?

Minimum payments are needs. Anything you pay above the minimum goes in the 20%.

### Is 50/30/20 realistic in an expensive city?

Often not for needs. Use it as a shape, try 60/20/20, and protect savings first.

### Where does an emergency fund go?

In the 20%. Most guidance suggests building it before investing extra.

### Can I use the rule with irregular income?

Yes, but base it on your lowest recent month, and put anything extra straight into savings.

## Bottom line

The 50/30/20 budget rule is a good first shape and a poor law. Apply it to take-home pay, compare it with what you really spend, and move the lines when your rent says so. Protecting the savings number matters more than hitting the other two exactly.
