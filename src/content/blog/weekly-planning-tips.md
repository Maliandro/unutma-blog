Monday morning arrives and the week is already ahead of you. Three things are overdue, a bill is due on Wednesday, and there is no milk. Most weekly planning tips assume you have an hour on Sunday and a love of colour-coded calendars. This one assumes you have twenty minutes and would rather spend them on something else.

Below is a timed checklist in four parts, with minutes for each. It works with paper, a calendar app or Unutma. The point is not a perfect plan. It is to start the week knowing the three things that matter and the two dates you cannot miss.

## Why a weekly review works

A plan made once a week catches problems while they are still small: a clash on Thursday, a bill before payday, a task that has been sitting for three weeks. It also reduces the background worry of unfinished things, because they are written down with a next step.

There is also some evidence for planning in the "if, then" format. A 2021 meta-analysis in [Frontiers in Psychology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8149892/) found a small-to-medium effect on goal attainment when people combined thinking about obstacles with specific if-then plans. The last step of the checklist below uses that format.

## The 20-minute weekly planning checklist

Same day, same time each week. Sunday evening and Friday afternoon are the most common choices.

### Part 1: look back (5 minutes)

- [ ] Scroll through last week's calendar. Anything that needs a follow-up?
- [ ] Check what you finished. Clear completed items from your lists.
- [ ] Note one thing that went well and one that did not. One line each.

### Part 2: clear the lists (5 minutes)

- [ ] Go through open to-dos. For each: do it this week, give it a date, or delete it.
- [ ] Anything older than three weeks gets a decision today. Most can go.
- [ ] Move loose notes and ideas into the right list.

### Part 3: dates and money (5 minutes)

- [ ] Check this week's appointments and set reminders for any without one.
- [ ] Check which bills are due before your next payday.
- [ ] Write the shopping list for the week's meals.

The US Consumer Financial Protection Bureau suggests mapping bill due dates against income dates; its [Your Money, Your Goals toolkit](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/educator-tools/your-money-your-goals/toolkit/) includes a free bill calendar for exactly that.

### Part 4: choose the week (5 minutes)

- [ ] Pick three priorities. Not ten. Three.
- [ ] Give each one a day.
- [ ] Write one if-then plan: "If Wednesday blows up, then the report moves to Thursday morning."
- [ ] Check that each routine still fits this week. Shorten any that will not.

## Mistakes that make weekly planning fail

1. **Planning the whole week by the hour.** Leave most of it open. The plan is three priorities and the fixed dates.
2. **Never deleting anything.** A list that only grows becomes a source of guilt. Delete freely in Part 2.
3. **Skipping the look-back.** Without it, the same problem repeats every week.
4. **Making it long.** If the review takes an hour, you will stop doing it by week four.

## When weekly planning is not enough

If your work changes every day, plan the week lightly and add a two-minute daily check of the first task. If you share a household calendar with someone, do Part 3 together. Unutma has no live shared lists, so a shared family calendar is better handled in a cloud app built for that.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which day works for weekly planning?

Whichever day you can protect. Sunday evening and Friday afternoon are common because they sit between the work week and the weekend.

### How long should a weekly review take?

Twenty minutes is enough for most people. If it regularly takes longer, your lists are too long.

### How many priorities should I set for a week?

Three. Everything else is a task that happens if there is time.

### Do I need a planner app?

No. Paper works. An app helps with reminders, recurring routines and seeing last week at a glance.

### What if I miss a week?

Do Part 2 and Part 4 only. Ten minutes gets you back on track.

## Bottom line

Good weekly planning tips all point the same way: short, regular and honest. Look back, clear the lists, check dates and money, pick three priorities. Twenty minutes on Sunday makes Monday a lot less loud.
