Most morning routine advice is written for people who wake up at 5am with nothing to do until 9. Real mornings involve a snooze button, someone else's breakfast and a bus. A routine that only works on a perfect day is not a routine. The fix is to plan the morning and evening routine as a pair, because a calm morning is mostly decided the night before.

Below is a two-column template you can copy, with a full version for normal days and a short version for bad ones. Then there is a quick look at what sleep guidance actually says, and how to set both routines up as reminders on your phone.

## Why plan mornings and evenings together

Three of the usual morning problems start the evening before: going to bed late, not knowing what tomorrow holds, and hunting for keys, cards or a charger. Moving those decisions to the evening shortens the morning without waking up earlier.

The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention lists a few [healthy sleep habits](https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/index.html) that fit neatly into an evening routine: keep a consistent schedule, going to bed and waking up at the same times each day; put devices away at least 30 minutes before bed; and avoid caffeine in the afternoon or evening.

## The two-column template

| | Morning | Evening |
|---|---|---|
| Anchor | Alarm goes off | You plug in your phone for the night |
| Step 1 | Drink a glass of water | Check tomorrow: calendar and first task |
| Step 2 | Take medication, if any | Put keys, wallet and bag in one place |
| Step 3 | Open the curtains or step outside for a minute | Lay out tomorrow's clothes |
| Step 4 | Look at today's first task, nothing else | Two-line journal entry or a mood tap |
| Step 5 | Leave the phone face down until you have eaten | Phone away 30 minutes before sleep |
| Short version (bad day) | Water and medication | Phone plugged in, keys in place |
| Time | 10 to 15 minutes | 10 to 15 minutes |

Change any step. Keep three things: an anchor you already do, a short version for bad days, and no more than five steps. A routine with twelve steps is a project.

## How to make it stick

Routines become automatic through repetition in the same situation. A review in the [British Journal of General Practice](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3505409/) describes how, in one study, daily actions took on average around 66 days to feel automatic, and that missing a single day did not seriously set people back. So:

- **Run the short version on bad days** instead of skipping. It keeps the cue and the action linked.
- **Do not add steps for the first month.** Expansion is the main way routines collapse.
- **Change one thing at a time** if something is not working.

## Setting both routines up in Unutma

The app has built-in templates for this, so you do not have to start from a blank screen.

1. **Open Reminders** and tap the templates button. There is a "Morning" template (Wake up, Take medication) and an "Evening" template (Wind down, Plan tomorrow). Apply them, then edit.
2. **Pick the days and a time** for each routine. Each routine can also be grouped under Morning, Afternoon, Evening or Anytime.
3. **Choose the reminder type.** Gentle for most steps, Alarm only for medication if you tend to miss it. "Add to phone alarm" copies the time to your phone's clock app.
4. **Use the evening for tomorrow.** "Note to Tomorrow" lets you leave one line that greets you the next morning. The Calendar shows tomorrow's events.
5. **Close the day.** The optional "Evening check-in" notification offers one evening line: a question, a memory from this day, or your note to tomorrow. A mood tap in the Journal counts as an entry.

There is also a "Morning routine" template for to-do lists (Wake up, Drink water, Plan the day) if you prefer a checklist to reminders. On the free plan you can have up to 3 routines or reminders, which covers a morning and an evening routine with one to spare.

## Who this template is not for

Shift workers and new parents will find "same time every day" unrealistic. Anchor the routine to an event instead, such as "after I get home" or "after the last feed", and keep the short version as the default. If your sleep is regularly poor despite a steady routine, that is a question for a doctor, not an app.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a morning routine be?

Ten to fifteen minutes is enough for most people. Longer routines are harder to keep on busy days.

### What should an evening routine include?

Three things help most: checking tomorrow, putting essentials in one place, and putting the phone away before sleep.

### Do I need to wake up early?

No. A routine is about order, not the clock. Start from the time you already wake up.

### What if I miss my routine?

Do the short version the next day. One missed day does not undo a routine.

### Should I use my phone in the morning routine?

For one thing: the first task of the day. Leave messages and news until you have eaten.

## Bottom line

A good morning and evening routine is two short lists that hand over to each other. Plan tomorrow tonight, keep five steps at most, and always have a short version. That is usually enough to make the morning feel lighter without setting an earlier alarm.
